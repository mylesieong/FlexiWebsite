A 10-minute daily stretching routine for beginners

Six stretches, held for 30 seconds each, in an order that works from the top of your body down. No equipment, no floor space beyond a mat's worth, no experience needed.

How long to hold a stretch

The single most common beginner mistake is holding for four or five seconds and moving on. That is long enough to feel a stretch and not long enough to change anything. Thirty seconds per stretch is the number worth remembering — long enough for the muscle to stop resisting and settle, short enough that a whole-body routine still fits in ten minutes.

Give yourself roughly fifteen seconds to get into each position before the clock starts. Rushing into a stretch is how people end up straining something on day one.

The maths: six stretches × (15 seconds getting in + 30 seconds holding) ≈ 4.5 minutes. Do the asymmetric ones on both sides and you land at roughly ten minutes — which is why Flexi's plans are built on that same timing.

The 10-minute routine

Work top to bottom. Each stretch feeds the next, and finishing on the floor means you are not bouncing up and down between positions.

30 seconds each side

1. Seated neck release

Sit tall. Drop your right ear towards your right shoulder and let the weight of your head do the work — no pulling. Keep the opposite shoulder down. You should feel a long, mild line down the left side of your neck.

30 seconds each side

2. Cross-body shoulder stretch

Bring your right arm straight across your chest and hook it with your left forearm. Pull it towards you gently, keeping the right shoulder down rather than shrugged up towards your ear.

30 seconds

3. Standing chest opener

Clasp your hands behind your back, straighten your arms, and lift them slightly while opening your chest. This is the direct counter to the rounded-shoulder position most of us sit in all day.

30 seconds each side

4. Kneeling hip flexor stretch

Kneel on your right knee with your left foot forward. Tuck your tailbone under and shift your weight gently forward. The stretch belongs at the front of your right hip — if you feel it in the knee, add padding underneath.

30 seconds each side

5. Lying hamstring stretch

Lie on your back. Lift one leg towards the ceiling, keeping it as straight as is comfortable, and hold behind the thigh. The other leg stays flat on the floor — letting it bend up quietly cheats the stretch.

30 seconds

6. Butterfly stretch

Sit with the soles of your feet together and knees falling open. Sit tall first, then hinge forward from the hips rather than rounding your back. A good, calm place to finish.

Getting the form right

  • Breathe out into the stretch. Holding your breath makes muscles guard. A slow exhale as you settle in gets you noticeably further.
  • Stop at tension, not pain. The right intensity is a firm pull you could hold a conversation through. Sharp, hot or nervy sensations mean back off.
  • Do not bounce. Bouncing triggers a reflex that tightens the muscle you are trying to lengthen.
  • Do both sides. Tempting to skip the side that feels fine. That is usually the side keeping you balanced.
  • Warm is easier than cold. After a walk, a shower or any activity, everything gives more readily — but a cold stretch done today beats a warm one you skip.

Making it a habit

Flexibility is a consistency game. Ten minutes six days a week beats an hour on a Sunday, comfortably. Three things make the difference in the first month:

  • Attach it to something you already do. After brushing your teeth, before the shower, right after you close the laptop. A time of day beats a vague intention.
  • Set one reminder, not five. One nudge at a time you are genuinely free is a prompt. Five is noise you will mute.
  • Count the days. A visible streak is a surprisingly effective reason not to skip a Tuesday — it turns an abstract habit into something you can lose.

Common questions

Should I stretch before or after exercise?

Save long static holds like these for after, or for a standalone session. Before a workout, a few minutes of moving warm-up — leg swings, arm circles, a brisk walk — prepares you better than holding a stretch for 30 seconds.

How soon will I notice a difference?

Most people feel looser within the same session. Lasting change in how far you can move usually shows up after three to four weeks of near-daily practice.

Is it bad if I am very inflexible?

No — it is just a starting point. Depth is irrelevant; what matters is that you feel a mild stretch in the right place. Bend the knee, shorten the reach, use a cushion. The muscle cannot tell how far you went.

Can I stretch every day?

Yes. Gentle daily stretching does not need recovery days the way heavy lifting does. If something feels genuinely sore rather than tight, give that area a day off.
